    01

    EE does two jobs

    Before the subfields, what is electrical engineering actually for?

    Builds on: nothing. Start here.
    Core

    Electrical engineers use electrons and electromagnetic fields to do two jobs: move energy (make it, convert it, deliver it) and move information (sense it, compute on it, send it). Nearly every concentration sits mostly on one side, and the most interesting ones sit where the two meet.

    Build

    EE and its neighbors

    • Computer engineering is EE plus computer science: the boundary where hardware meets software. Many schools treat it as an EE track.
    • Computer science stays above the hardware. A CS grad assumes the chip works; an EE makes it work.
    • Mechanical + EE is mechatronics and robotics: motors, sensors, control loops.
    • Physics discovers the effects; EE turns them into products that can be built a billion times.
    Context

    Five moments that made the field

    • 1880s: Edison and Westinghouse fight over DC vs AC power. AC wins because transformers make long-distance transmission cheap.
    • 1947: the transistor at Bell Labs replaces vacuum tubes.
    • 1958–59: the integrated circuit puts many transistors on one chip.
    • 1971: the first single-chip microprocessor (Intel 4004).
    • 2020s: two booms at once: AI chips on the information side, and grid, EV and data-center power on the energy side.
    ExampleOne phone call touches almost every concentration

    You speak. A MEMS microphone (devices) turns sound into a tiny voltage. An analog front end and ADC (analog circuits) digitize it. A DSP removes background noise (signal processing). The application processor (digital IC design) compresses it, under control of firmware (embedded). The RF front end and antenna (RF, electromagnetics) send it to a tower using a 5G standard (communications). It rides fiber (photonics) across the country. The whole time, a battery-management chip and the grid behind the charger (power) keep it alive.

    Recall check

    Is a power engineer at a utility on the energy side or the information side?

    Energy. They care about efficiency, safety and reliability at megawatt scale.

    Why did AC win the 1880s "war of the currents"?

    Transformers can step AC voltage up for low-loss long-distance transmission and back down for homes. DC couldn't do that cheaply at the time.

    02

    The stack: every EE works on a rung

    How do thousands of engineers work on one product without each understanding all of it?

    Builds on: 01Two jobs
    Core

    EE is organized as a stack of abstraction layers: physics → devices → circuits → chips and boards → systems → networks and grids. Each layer hides the details of the one below, so a digital designer can trust that a gate works without thinking about electrons. A concentration is a range of rungs on this stack.

    Explore

    Where each concentration lives on the stack

    Each bar spans the rungs a concentration works on. Click a bar or a name to see what it covers. Notice how few reach all the way down to physics, and how few reach all the way up to systems.

    Build

    Why the stack matters to you

    Most problems are solved inside one layer. The hard ones cross layers: a chip that fails only when it gets hot, a board that crashes when a motor starts. The engineers people value most are comfortable one layer above and one below their own, so they can tell where a problem really lives.

    ExampleDropping down a layer to find a bug

    A laptop reboots at random (system symptom). Software engineers find nothing. A hardware engineer probes the board and sees the processor's supply voltage dip when the GPU wakes up. At the circuit layer the cause is a voltage regulator whose control loop responds too slowly to the sudden load. The fix is a change in one capacitor value. The person who found it understood three rungs, not one.

    Recall check

    What does an abstraction layer "hide"?

    The details of the layer below. A logic designer works with gates and timing, not with electron mobility.

    Which concentration spans the widest range of the stack?

    Usually embedded systems or controls: they touch circuits and boards but also whole systems. Devices and EM reach down to physics instead.

    03

    The concentrations

    What does each specialty actually build, and who hires for it?

    Builds on: 01Two jobs 02The stack
    Core

    There are about ten concentrations. Group them by side: energy (power), information hardware (devices, analog, digital, embedded), information signals (signal processing, communications and RF, electromagnetics), and the bridges (controls, biomedical). Most undergrads take the same core courses and then pick one or two.

    EnergyMoving power
    InformationBuilding the hardware
    InformationShaping and sending signals
    BridgesWhere energy and information meet
    Context

    Concentrations blur in real jobs

    Job titles rarely match course names. "Hardware engineer" at a consumer company might mean board design plus embedded plus a little RF. "Electrical engineer" at a utility means power. When reading a job post, look at the tools and verbs ("SPICE", "Verilog", "protection relays", "firmware") to tell which concentration it really is.

    Recall check

    An engineer writes SystemVerilog tests all day for an AI chip. Which concentration?

    Digital and computer hardware, specifically design verification.

    Which two concentrations would design a phone's 5G antenna and the chip that drives it?

    Electromagnetics (the antenna) and RF/communications (the transceiver and its standard). Analog circuits overlap heavily too.

    04

    Chips up close: the IC abstraction layers

    The chip industry employs more EEs than any other. How is a chip actually made, and who does each part?

    Builds on: 02The stack 03Digital & devices EE 10Fab & IC design
    Core

    A chip is designed top-down through five abstraction levels (System → RTL → Logic → Circuit → Layout), then built bottom-up in a fab, one layer of material at a time. Each level has its own job titles and tools. The layout is sent to the fab as a set of mask patterns: that hand-off is called tape-out.

    Explore

    IC design abstraction levels

    Each level is wider than the one above because it holds more detail: one "CPU" block at the top becomes billions of polygons at the bottom. Click a level to see who works there.

    Build

    Front end vs back end

    • Front end (System, RTL, Logic): decide what the chip does and prove it's correct. Mostly code and simulation.
    • Back end (physical design): turn the gates into placed, wired, timing-correct layout.
    • Analog and custom designers skip RTL and work at Circuit and Layout by hand, because amplifiers and ADCs can't be synthesized from code.
    • Verification often takes more engineers than design. A bug found after tape-out can cost months and millions of dollars.
    Build

    The PDK: the contract between design and fab

    The fab gives designers a Process Design Kit: the design rules (minimum widths and spacings), the transistor compact models for SPICE, and standard-cell libraries. Designers never see the fab's recipes; process engineers never see the chip's purpose. The PDK is the abstraction layer between the two companies.

    The semiconductor value chain

    ExampleHow a phone's main chip gets made

    Apple's architects define the chip and license the Arm instruction set. Apple's RTL, verification and physical-design teams build it using Synopsys and Cadence tools. The layout is taped out to TSMC, which prints it on wafers in Taiwan using ASML EUV machines (ASML is the only company that makes them). The dies are packaged and tested, then shipped to phone assembly lines. One chip, at least five companies, several countries.

    ExampleAn analog chip at an IDM

    At Texas Instruments, a team designs a battery-charger chip. The circuit designer sizes transistors in Cadence Virtuoso and simulates them with TI's own process models. A layout engineer draws it by hand. It's made in a TI-owned fab, tested on TI's test floor, and sold through a catalog of tens of thousands of parts. Analog chips sell for years with little change, so the work is slower-paced and more stable than leading-edge digital.

    Context

    Why chips are in the news

    Most of the world's leading-edge logic is made in Taiwan, and the machines that make it come from a handful of companies. That concentration is why governments subsidize new fabs (the 2022 U.S. CHIPS Act funded projects in Arizona, Texas, Ohio and New York) and restrict exports of advanced chips and tools. It also makes the industry cyclical: shortages, then gluts.

    Recall check

    Nvidia designs GPUs but doesn't own a fab. What kind of company is it?

    Fabless. Its chips are made by a foundry, mainly TSMC.

    Why can't an op-amp be written in Verilog and synthesized?

    Synthesis maps logic onto standard digital gates. Analog circuits depend on exact transistor sizes, matching and bias, so they're designed at the circuit and layout level by hand.

    What is tape-out?

    The moment the finished layout is sent to the fab to make masks. After that, changes are expensive.

    05

    How the industries work

    Chips are one industry. Where else do EEs work, and how does each one feel from the inside?

    Builds on: 03Concentrations 04Chips
    Core

    Who pays for the work shapes the job. Consumer markets are fast and cost-driven. Government customers (defense, space) are slow, document-heavy and stable. Regulated utilities are conservative and safety-first. The same EE skill can feel completely different depending on which of these pays your salary.

    Recall check

    Which industry is most likely to require U.S. citizenship and a security clearance?

    Aerospace and defense.

    Why is power engineering hiring growing right now?

    Data centers for AI, EV charging, and renewable generation all need new grid connections, substations and transmission. Many experienced power engineers are also retiring.

    Which concentration shows up in almost every industry?

    Embedded systems. Anything with a microcontroller needs firmware and boards.

    06

    Roles: what the job actually is

    Two EEs in the same concentration at the same company can have completely different days. Why?

    Builds on: 04Chips 05Industries
    Core

    Your concentration says what you work on. Your role says when in the product's life you touch it: research → design → verify → test → manufacture → support. Design is the famous role, but verification, test, applications and systems roles hire many EEs and are common first jobs.

    ExampleOne data converter chip, five jobs

    A company makes a new analog-to-digital converter. A design engineer creates the circuit. A layout engineer draws it. A test engineer writes the program that checks every chip off the line in under a second. An applications engineer builds an evaluation board and writes the datasheet examples. A product marketer decided two years earlier which specs customers would pay for. All five are EEs.

    Context

    Moving between roles is normal

    Test and applications engineers often move into design after a few years, because they've seen how real parts fail. Designers often move into systems or management later. The first role is a starting rung, not a life sentence.

    Recall check

    Before tape-out, who proves a digital chip is correct? After it's built?

    Before: verification engineers (simulation). After: validation and test engineers (real silicon in the lab).

    Which role is most customer-facing?

    Applications or field engineer (and sales engineer).

    07

    Getting in: degrees, licenses, skills

    Given everything above, what does it take to get into each part of the field?

    Builds on: 03Concentrations 06Roles
    Core

    A BS in EE is the standard ticket, and internships or co-ops matter most for the first job. A master's is common for IC design, RF and signal processing. A PhD is for research and device physics. The PE license matters mainly in power and building systems.

    CredentialWhere it matters mostNotes

    MSAnalog/RF IC design, DSP, communications, some verificationOften paid for by employers; many do it part-time

    Build

    Tools that show up in job posts

    • Everyone: Python or MATLAB, oscilloscope, multimeter, reading datasheets
    • Circuits: SPICE (LTspice, Cadence Spectre), PCB tools (KiCad, Altium)
    • Digital: Verilog or SystemVerilog, FPGA boards, Linux scripting
    • Embedded: C on microcontrollers, debuggers, logic analyzers
    • RF: spectrum analyzer, network analyzer (VNA), EM simulators
    • Power: load-flow and protection software (ETAP, PSS/E), AutoCAD

    ExampleTurning coursework into resume lines
    • Digital: implement a small CPU or a UART on an FPGA and put the Verilog on GitHub.
    • Circuits: design and order a two-layer PCB in KiCad (a sensor board or a small power supply) and measure it.
    • Embedded: a microcontroller project that reads a sensor, sleeps to save battery, and talks over Bluetooth.
    • Power: a solar charge controller, or a load-flow study in a class tool.

    One finished, measured project says more than three half-done ones.

    Recall check

    You want to design analog chips. BS, MS or PhD?

    An MS is the most common entry point for analog and RF IC design, though strong BS grads with good internships do get in.

    Which concentration cares most about the PE license?

    Power, especially at utilities and consulting firms where a PE must stamp designs.
